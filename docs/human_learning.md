# Trial-by-trial models of human adjustment: revised protocol and analysis specification

Module: `heiner_abm/human_learning.py`. Tests: `tests/test_human_learning.py` (47).
Results: `docs/human_learning_results/`. Plan `a76705ab2c4662ba`. Reproduce: `python tools/run_human_learning.py`.

> **Everything in this note is synthetic.** The participants are generated by the models being fitted. The
> results say whether this design *could* identify these mechanisms if people behaved this way. They are not
> evidence that anybody does, and no number here is a finding about human behaviour. The registered human study
> has not been run.

---

## What was missing

`human_models` fits four hurdle models — inaction band, probabilistic adjustment, inertia, rounding. They are
useful and they are not learning models. Two things limit them:

- **They are static.** The probability of adjusting is a function of the current signal; nothing carries from one
  decision to the next. They describe a decision rule, not a process that acquires anything.
- **They are scored against a quantity nobody saw.** The signal is `shadow:opt_br`, the researcher's filtered
  best reply, which `human_models` itself documents as *never shown*. A model fitted to it can predict choices
  well while describing a computation the participant could not perform.

The five models here are learning processes, and every input is on the screen.

## What the participant sees

`stepper.InteractiveMarket.info()` *is* the screen. Before deciding in period *t*:

| | |
|---|---|
| `cost_estimate` | ĉ_t — a noisy estimate of this period's unit cost |
| `last_cost` | c_{t−1} — the cost that actually applied |
| `last_price` | P_{t−1} |
| `own_profit` | π_{t−1} |
| `own_q` | q_{t−1} |
| `last_market_q` | Q_{t−1} |

and nothing else. Two derived quantities are built only from that list: the cost surprise `u_t = ĉ_t − c_{t−1}`,
the one forward-looking number on the screen, and realized profit as the one outcome signal.

A test asserts no column access matching `shadow:` exists anywhere in the module, and a second deletes every
shadow column from the data and checks the prepared arrays and the fits are unchanged.

## The five models

All share a lapse rate and a magnitude model — Δ ~ N(−φ·u_t, sd²) on whole units, conditioned on Δ ≠ 0 — so
model comparison compares **adaptation decisions** and not how large a change somebody makes once they have
decided to make one. φ is free, so a participant who ignores the cost estimate has φ = 0.

| model | state | update | choice rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **advantage** | V_adjust, V_keep | delta rule on realized profit for the action taken | logistic(a₀ + β·m) |
| **confidence** | V and their variances v | Kalman; both variances drift, only the taken action's shrinks | logistic(a₀ + β·m − ω·U) |
| **changebayes** | μ, s² over the cost level | Bayes on ĉ_t: posterior change probability raises the gain | logistic(a₀ + β·surprise + λ·r) |
| **aspiration** | A, an aspiration on profit | A ← A + η(π_{t−1} − A) | logistic(a₀ + β·(A − π_{t−1})) |
| **risk** | as confidence, plus w, an EW variance of profit | as confidence | logistic(a₀ + β·m − ω·U − γ·√w) |

with m = V_adjust − V_keep and U = √(v_adjust + v_keep).

**ω = 0 reduces `confidence` to `advantage`**, so confidence sensitivity is a test on one parameter. **ω and γ
are signed.** Constraining them positive would make "is there confidence sensitivity?" unanswerable — every
estimate would support it — and a negative ω is a real possibility worth being able to see: adapting *more* when
uncertain is exploration, not a bug.

A sixth model, **`inertia_learn`** (advantage + a stickiness term on having kept last period), is not one of the
five. It is the competitor they have to be told apart from, in the same family and on the same observables,
because inertia predicts "adapts less after keeping" and confidence sensitivity predicts "adapts less when
uncertain", and early in a block those look nearly the same.

## Block boundaries

Every state resets at each block: values, variances, aspiration and cost belief. Blocks are different markets
with different cost paths, presented as such, so carrying a learned value across one would be a claim about the
participant that the protocol does not support. Tests replace the whole of block 1 with noise and assert the
signals of later blocks do not move, and check for every model that `reset()` restores a fresh learner exactly.
`CARRY_STATE = False` documents the switch a continuous-environment protocol would have to flip.

## The four manipulations

| manipulation | factor | moves | leaves alone | new |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| outcome variability | condition V (δ 8→24) | spread of realized profit | precision of the cost estimate | |
| observation precision | condition N (noise 2→10) | precision of the cost estimate | spread of the world | |
| **sampling history** | 0 or 10 forced-sampling periods | estimation uncertainty at the first scored decision | everything on screen at the moment of choice | ✓ |
| **further learning** | 12 or 32 scored periods, announced | the value of adapting now | the screen and the precision of the estimate | ✓ |

The last two exist because confidence sensitivity, inertia, risk preference and slow learning *all* predict
"adapts less", and the two registered conditions cannot separate them — V and N both change what is on screen at
the moment of choice. Sampling history changes the **precision** of the estimate while leaving the screen
identical; the horizon changes the **value** of adapting while leaving both screen and precision identical.

### Two false starts, recorded because they are the point

> **A passive lead-in does not work.** The first version held output and let the participant watch. That only
> ever samples *keep*, and in an action-value learner the uncertainty about adjusting falls only when adjusting
> is tried. Measured: uncertainty at the first scored decision moved 1.414 → 1.321, and the block mean did not
> move at all. The lead-in now alternates forced keeps and forced changes, and the same number is 1.414 → 0.728.
>
> **Placement matters more than it looks.** With every forced period at the front, the uncertainty signal is a
> monotone decay in trial index — nearly collinear with the intercept, so ω cannot be told from a participant who
> simply adapts less often. Splitting it into two bouts, one at the start and one mid-block, raised ω recovery
> from r = 0.580 to 0.669.

### Counterbalancing

Conditions stay on the Williams square. The order-4 balanced square is built on the cyclic group and therefore
has **no orthogonal mate**, so there is no second square crossing conditions with factor cells within a single
participant. Rotating the factor cell by the **arm** index instead gives a design where every condition meets
every factor cell exactly once within each arm and four times overall — a test counts both rather than trusting
this paragraph.

## Analysis specification

- **Participant-level dependence is kept.** Every parameter is estimated per participant by two-pass empirical
  Bayes; the population enters only through the prior. Pooling decisions into one regression would treat ~88
  decisions from one person as independent observations.
- **Held out by block, never by trial.** The decisions of a block share a learning trajectory; leaving out single
  trials would let the fit see the state that produced them. The prior for a participant's held-out fit is
  computed without that participant.
- **Forced-sampling periods are kept in the data, and scored as evidence rather than as choices.** They advance
  the state and contribute nothing to the likelihood. Dropping them — the first implementation — makes the fitted
  learner start every block from its prior and silently erases the sampling-history manipulation while leaving
  the generated data unchanged.
- **Model comparison** by held-out log score, with BIC reported beside it.
- **The test of confidence sensitivity** is a two-sided one-sample *t*-test on the per-participant ω̂ against
  zero: a test on participants, not on decisions.

> **The "weak" prior was not weak.** At prior sd 1.0, two-pass empirical Bayes shrank ω from a true mean of 0.92
> to an estimate of 0.25 — while leaving the correlation with the truth at 0.67 either way. A rank-based recovery
> check would have passed and reported a recovered parameter with a badly wrong magnitude. The prior spread on
> the two signed parameters is now 2.5, which leaves the correlation unchanged and the bias at −0.08.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| a0 | 0.89 | 0.45 | 0.87 | 0.91 | 0.48 | 0.81 |
| beta | 0.81 | 0.75 | 0.73 | 0.69 | 0.43 | 0.41 |
| **omega** | — | **0.49** | — | — | **0.21** | — |
| gamma | — | — | — | — | 0.45 | — |
| alpha / eta / g | 0.30 | — | — | 0.73 | — | 0.69 / 0.64 |
| sd | 0.92 | 0.85 | 0.90 | 0.88 | 0.68 | 0.86 |
| phi | 0.43 | 0.33 | 0.30 | 0.65 | 0.25 | 0.42 |

The magnitude parameters recover well (sd ≥ 0.68 everywhere). The **mechanism** parameters are mixed:
`aspiration`'s adaptation rate (0.73) and `inertia_learn`'s stickiness (0.64) are usable; `changebayes`'s
believed hazard (0.15) and baseline gain (0.13) are not estimable at this length at all, and `risk` degrades
across the board — with eleven free parameters it is the most demanding model and the least identified.

Within `confidence`, ω reaches 0.49 in this run and **0.70 in the targeted probe** below; the difference is that
the probe uses the split forced-sampling placement throughout, and the gap between the two is itself the measure
of what that design choice buys.

| inertia_learn | 0.10 | 0.20 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.20 | **0.50** |

**Only `aspiration` is cleanly recovered** (0.90). It is the one model whose driver — profit relative to a
moving reference — is not a function of the action-value state, so nothing else in the family can imitate it.

The four models built on action values (`advantage`, `confidence`, `risk`, `inertia_learn`) are **badly confused
with each other**: diagonals of 0.30–0.50, and data generated by `advantage` is assigned to `inertia_learn` more
often (0.40) than to itself (0.30). At ~88 decisions per participant this design cannot tell apart models that
differ only in how the same learned advantage is converted into a choice. That is a limit of the protocol, not
of the estimator, and it is the single most important number in this note for anyone planning to run it.

### Can confidence sensitivity be told apart? (the brief's question)

| | claim | result | supported |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I1** | ω is recoverable | r = 0.703 | yes |
| **I2** | ω is not inertia | confidence selected 28%, inertia 16% | yes, weakly |
| **I3** | ω is not risk preference | error correlation −0.15 | yes |
| **I4** | ω is not slow learning | ω gap +0.018 against s.d. 0.415 | **no** |
| **I5** | the new manipulations help | r = 0.703 with, 0.497 without | yes |

**I4 fails, and the detail matters.** A *confident waiter* (ω = 1.2, precise) and a *slow learner* (ω = 0,
σ_r = 6), matched so they adapt at nearly the same rate (0.244 vs 0.300), produce virtually identical ω
estimates — 0.734 against 0.715. The confidence parameter cannot separate them.

But they are not indistinguishable: their estimated **σ_r differs clearly, 0.75 against 1.45**. The design
separates these two profiles through the learner's assumed outcome noise, not through its confidence
sensitivity. Any analysis that reads ω alone will call a slow learner confidence-sensitive.

**I2 is supported but thin.** On data generated with ω > 0 and no inertia at all, `confidence` is selected for
only 28% of participants and `inertia_learn` for 16% — but `risk` takes 38%, more than either. The ordering is
right; the margin is not one to rely on.

**I5 holds**: ω recovery rises from 0.497 to 0.703 when the sampling-history and horizon factors are present.
The two new manipulations earn their place.

### Power, and why the first answer was wrong

The first power analysis was invalid, and the thing that caught it was the null row beside it. **The test
rejected at 0.50–0.75 against a nominal 0.05.** A procedure that fires on half of all pure-noise samples has no
power curve worth quoting, so the original numbers are kept below as a diagnosis rather than deleted.

| procedure | n = 16 | n = 32 | n = 64 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **as originally specified** — two-pass estimates, shared paths | null **0.50** / power 0.67 | null **0.50** / power 0.92 | null **0.50** / power 1.00 |
| first-pass estimates, shared paths | null 0.08 / power 0.42 | null 0.17 / power **0.92** | null 0.08 / power 1.00 |
| first-pass estimates, paths re-randomised | null 0.08 / power 0.33 | null 0.17 / power 0.75 | null 0.08 / power 1.00 |

**Cause 1 — two-pass pooling breaks the test, and this is the whole problem.** Empirical Bayes shrinks the
*spread* of the per-participant estimates (s.d. 1.0 → 0.3–0.5 under the null) while leaving the common offset of
a replication untouched. A *t*-statistic divides that offset by the shrunken spread, so it rejects. The
estimates share a prior fitted to the same data and are not independent observations. Using the first-pass
estimates, which are fitted against a fixed prior, takes the false-positive rate from 0.50 to 0.08–0.17.

**Cause 2 — the shared stimulus, which turned out not to matter here.** The registered protocol gives every
participant the same four cost paths, so a path favouring patience moves the whole sample together: measured
under the null, the across-replication s.d. of the mean estimate is 0.374 with shared paths and 0.220 when they
are re-randomised. That is a real dependence, and it does not shrink with sample size. **But re-randomising did
not improve calibration at all** — the null rates are identical to three decimals — and it *cost* power
(0.92 → 0.75 at n = 32), because varying the stimulus between participants adds between-participant variance to
the estimate. The prediction that it would help was wrong, and it is reported as wrong rather than quietly
dropped.

**What can and cannot be concluded.** With 12 replications the Monte-Carlo standard error on a rejection rate
near 0.1 is about 0.08, so 0.08 and 0.17 are not distinguishable from each other or from 0.05. The corrected
procedure is **consistent with nominal calibration and has not been shown to achieve it**; confirming that needs
several hundred replications, which this run did not do. What is established is the negative: the procedure as
originally specified is definitively broken, at 0.50 against a standard error of 0.14.

**Planning recommendation, conditional on that check.** First-pass estimates, shared paths, **n ≈ 32–48**: power
0.92 at n = 32 for ω = 0.8 and 1.00 by n = 64. Do not use the pooled estimates for the population test — they
are the right estimates for describing a participant and the wrong ones for testing a population.

### What this does and does not show

Six models, one market, synthetic participants, one frozen plan. Every number is about whether this *design*
could identify these mechanisms in data that behaves exactly as the models say. Nobody has been run. The
honest summary for anyone planning the study: `aspiration` is separable, the action-value family is not at this
length, confidence sensitivity is recoverable but is not separable from slow learning through ω itself, and the
participant-level test needs re-randomised stimuli before any of it can be tested.
